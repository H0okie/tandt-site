The Lowcountry's sandy soil drains quickly, summer temperatures push past 90°F for months, and salt exposure stresses plants that aren't getting consistent moisture. A well-designed drip system puts water exactly where roots need it — slowly, efficiently, and without the waste of traditional sprinklers. ## Installation and custom design

We install complete drip systems for flower beds, vegetable gardens, foundation plantings, and container arrangements, starting with a property assessment of soil, sun, root zones, and water pressure. Because sandy Lowcountry soil drains fast, plants can dry out between traditional watering cycles — drip solves this by delivering water slowly and directly to root zones, reducing evaporation and runoff. Our process includes trenching supply lines, placing emitters at precise intervals, connecting to your water source, and programming timers, using commercial-grade tubing rated for Lowcountry heat and UV. Every property is different, so we design each system from scratch based on plant species, spacing, drainage rates, and water availability, delivering a scaled layout with emitter placement, zone mapping, and flow calculations and a written scope before installation.

## Micro and low-volume systems

Micro irrigation uses low-flow micro-sprayers, micro-bubblers, and drip emitters operating at much lower pressure than standard heads — ideal for mixed beds where different plants need different amounts of water, and using 30–50% less water than conventional sprinklers. Mount Pleasant homeowners with raised beds, herb gardens, and perennial borders benefit from dedicated drip lines with inline emitters spaced to their planting layout, which keeps water off foliage to reduce fungal disease in the humid climate; we also retrofit existing beds on properties that already have turf sprinklers. Low-volume systems deliver water in gallons per hour rather than per minute, letting sandy soil absorb it before it drains past the root zone — effective for trees, shrubs, hedge rows, and newly installed landscapes during establishment.

## Repair and maintenance

Drip systems need regular attention — emitters clog from mineral buildup in Charleston's water, tubing cracks after years of UV, rodents chew lines, and settling soil shifts emitters away from roots. We provide seasonal inspections, emitter cleaning and replacement, line repair, filter changes, and timer reprogramming, and adjust systems as your landscape matures, since what a new planting needed at install changes as roots expand.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why is drip irrigation important in Charleston?

It delivers water slowly and directly to roots, reducing evaporation and runoff. In sandy soil that drains quickly and summers above 90°F, drip prevents plants from drying out and wastes far less water than sprinklers, lowering your bills.

### How much water can it save?

Micro and drip systems use 30–50% less water than conventional sprinklers — the slow delivery rate lets sandy soil absorb water before it drains past the root zone.

### Why does drip reduce fungal disease?

It keeps water off foliage by delivering moisture to the soil. In the Lowcountry's humid environment wet leaves promote fungal growth, so watering only the soil reduces disease risk while improving plant health.

### How often does a drip system need maintenance?

Regular seasonal inspections — emitters clog, tubing cracks from UV, and settling soil shifts emitter positions. Our maintenance contracts include emitter cleaning, line repair, filter changes, and timer adjustments to keep systems efficient year-round.
