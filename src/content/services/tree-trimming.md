## Tree trimming service

Professional trimming removes dead, damaged, or overgrown branches to improve a tree's structure and reduce risk. For Charleston's live oaks, palmettos, crepe myrtles, and Southern magnolias, our service includes crown cleaning, deadwood removal, branch thinning, and clearance from roofs, fences, and power lines. We trim to industry standards — proper cuts at branch collars, no flush cuts, and never topping, which weakens structure and causes unstable regrowth. For HOAs and apartment complexes with dozens of trees, we build a trimming rotation into a recurring maintenance contract.

## Tree pruning and health assessments

Pruning is more targeted than general trimming — removing crossing branches, correcting poor crotch angles, thinning dense canopies for airflow, or elevating limbs for clearance. Young trees benefit most: a few well-placed cuts on a five-year-old tree prevent expensive structural problems a decade later, and for mature trees, selective pruning reduces wind resistance during storm season. We prune to ANSI A300 standards, assessing each tree by species, age, health, and location. Our health assessments identify early signs of fungal infection, scale insects, boring beetles, and overgrown Spanish moss weighing down limbs — with honest recommendations, not upsells.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the best time of year to trim trees in Charleston?

Late winter (February through early March) is ideal for most species — trees are dormant, which reduces stress and limits pest exposure at cut sites. Dead or hazardous branches should be removed any time, and storm cleanup can't wait for a calendar date.

### Do I need a permit to trim trees in Charleston?

The City has a tree protection ordinance for certain trees by species and trunk diameter — particularly grand trees (live oaks 24 inches or larger). Trimming typically doesn't require a permit, but removal often does; we can help you determine if your project is regulated.

### How often should trees be trimmed here?

Most mature trees benefit from professional trimming every two to three years. Fast-growing species like crepe myrtles or sweetgums may need annual attention, and trees near power lines or rooflines may need more frequent trimming for safety.
