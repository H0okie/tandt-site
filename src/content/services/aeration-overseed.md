## Core aeration in Charleston, SC

Core aeration is the foundation of every healthy lawn program. A machine with hollow tines pulls small plugs of soil — typically two to three inches deep — so air, water, and fertilizer can reach the root zone directly.

Charleston soils vary by neighborhood: sandy soils near the coast drain fast but compact under traffic, while clay-heavy areas in parts of North Charleston and West Ashley hold moisture but restrict roots. Core aeration addresses both by breaking up the compacted layer and creating space for roots to expand. We use commercial-grade walk-behind and ride-on aerators and adjust tine depth and pass frequency to your soil's actual condition.

## Overseeding services in Charleston, SC

Overseeding fills bare spots and thickens thin turf by working new seed into the existing lawn. It works best right after aeration, when seed falls into the freshly pulled holes and makes direct contact with soil. For cool-season lawns we typically use heat- and humidity-tolerant tall fescue blends; warm-season lawns like Bermuda and Zoysia spread on their own, but targeted overseeding accelerates recovery in damaged or high-traffic areas. We select seed by your grass type, sun exposure, and how the yard is used.

## Seasonal timing

Most lawns need aeration once a year; heavily compacted or high-traffic properties may benefit from twice-annual service. Warm-season grasses (Bermuda, Zoysia, Centipede, St. Augustine) are best aerated from late spring through mid-summer during active growth. Cool-season grasses like tall fescue respond best to fall aeration, roughly September through October. We send reminders before each service window so you never miss the optimal timing.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the best time to aerate a lawn in Charleston?

It depends on grass type. Warm-season grasses (Bermuda, Zoysia, Centipede) should be aerated during active growth from late spring through summer; cool-season grasses like tall fescue do best with fall aeration.

### Why is core aeration better than spike aeration?

Core aeration uses hollow tines to pull plugs of soil, physically removing compacted material and creating channels for air, water, and nutrients. Spike aeration just pokes holes, which can increase compaction. Clemson University recommends core aeration for long-term turf health.

### Can I mow right after aeration?

Wait two to three days before mowing. The soil plugs break down naturally within one to two weeks and return nutrients to the soil; a light watering after service helps.
