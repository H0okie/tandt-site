Yard drainage in Charleston, SC is one of the most common and most overlooked property issues in the Lowcountry. A high water table, flat coastal terrain, and sandy soil layered over clay create conditions where water has nowhere to go after heavy rain — puddles sit for days, mulch washes out, foundation walls stay damp, and hurricane season brings accelerated erosion to properties without a plan. Founded by Timothy Fowler and based at 2124 Parsons Corner, T&T designs, installs, and maintains yard drainage systems that move water off your property efficiently, protecting your home, your landscape investment, and your turf.

## Design, wet-yard fixes, and standing water

Every effective project starts with a site assessment — we evaluate grade, soil composition, water table depth, and existing runoff before recommending a system. Charleston's soil, often sandy topsoil over compacted clay, percolates quickly through the top layer, hits clay, and pools, so each design includes pipe sizing, slope calculations, and connection points mapped to your specific lot. A wet yard is more than an inconvenience — saturated soil kills grass roots, breeds mosquitoes, and grows mold near foundations, so we combine surface grading, subsurface drains, and water diversion via dry creek beds, swales, and pipe that carries runoff to the street, storm drain, or a low area. In Mount Pleasant subdivisions on reclaimed marsh or fill, where the water table sits just a few feet below grade, we fix standing water with targeted drains, localized regrading, and pump-assisted systems where gravity drainage isn't possible.

## Downspouts, grading, basins, and pumps

Roof runoff is one of the biggest contributors — a single inch of rain on a 2,000-square-foot roof produces over 1,200 gallons, so we install underground downspout extensions that carry water 15–30 feet or more from the home and tie into the broader system for one controlled exit path. Sometimes the problem is the grade itself: as soil settles and beds compact, the original slope flattens, so we reslope around foundations, patios, and beds to restore positive drainage — the first step in any new install. Catch basins collect surface water at low points and route it underground, while channel drains sit flush with driveways, patios, and pool decks to intercept sheet flow, both often required to meet commercial stormwater codes. For lots so low that gravity alone won't work — common near tidal zones and flood-prone areas of James Island and downtown — we install sump and drainage pumps rated for continuous outdoor use, with check valves and backup power where needed.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do I fix poor drainage in my yard?

Start by identifying where water collects and where it needs to go. Common fixes include French drains, regrading slopes, extending downspouts, and installing catch basins — a professional site assessment is the fastest way to determine the right solution for your property.

### Is a French drain better than a ditch?

French drains work underground and suit residential properties where appearance matters; open ditches or swales handle larger volumes and suit commercial or rural sites. On many Charleston lots, a French drain paired with proper grading delivers the best results.

### How much is the stormwater fee in Charleston?

The City charges a stormwater utility fee based on impervious surface, with tiered residential rates — you can check your specific fee through the City's Stormwater Division. Proper drainage can help manage runoff and reduce erosion.

### What causes standing water in Charleston yards?

The high water table, flat terrain, and dense clay-over-sand soil trap water at the surface, and heavy hurricane-season rainfall compounds it. Without a system directing water away from the home, standing water becomes a recurring issue on most Lowcountry properties.
