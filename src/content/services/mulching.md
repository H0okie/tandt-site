## Mulching service and delivery

Our process starts with a walkthrough to assess beds, weed issues, and mulch depth. We remove old, decomposed mulch when needed, pull weeds, and prepare the surface before spreading fresh material evenly at 2–3 inches — enough to suppress weeds and retain moisture without suffocating roots — and we keep mulch pulled back from trunks and plant bases to prevent rot and pests. We install hardwood mulch, dyed mulch (black, brown, red), pine bark, and pine straw. We also supply and deliver bulk mulch and install it the same day in most cases, which is far more cost-effective than bagged mulch for larger properties. We handle jobs from small residential beds to commercial installs of 50+ cubic yards.

## Bed edging and decorative mulch

Clean bed edges define the boundary between lawn and beds — without them, grass creeps into mulch and mulch washes into turf. We cut a sharp, defined line with professional edging tools, creating a trench that holds mulch in place, and can reshape neglected beds to improve the overall layout. For high-visibility areas — front-entry beds, mailboxes, walkways, and HOA common areas — we install decorative dyed hardwood and natural pine bark, helping you choose color and texture based on your home's exterior, plant material, and sun exposure (some dyed mulches fade faster in direct sun).

## Mulch refresh and top-dressing

Organic mulch decomposes noticeably within 6–12 months in Charleston's warm, humid climate. A refresh adds a thin layer of fresh material over existing mulch to restore color, maintain depth, and extend the life of your beds. We offer seasonal refresh programs — typically a spring refresh after winter and a light fall top-dressing — which are especially popular with property managers and HOAs who need consistent curb appeal across multiple buildings.

## Frequently asked questions

### How often should mulch be refreshed in Charleston?

Organic mulch decomposes within 6–12 months in this climate. A spring refresh after winter and a light fall top-dressing maintain proper depth and color year-round.

### What types of mulch do you install?

Hardwood mulch, dyed mulch (black, brown, red), pine bark nuggets, and pine straw — chosen for aesthetic preference, plant material, sun exposure, and soil conditions.

### Why is professional bed edging important?

It creates a sharp, defined boundary that stops grass from creeping into beds and mulch from washing into turf — reducing maintenance and keeping the landscape looking finished.

### How does sandy soil affect mulch needs?

Sandy soil drains quickly and salt exposure accelerates decomposition, so proper depth, material selection, and regular refreshing are essential to retain moisture and protect plants.
