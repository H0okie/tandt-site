## Shrub trimming and shaping

Shrub trimming keeps individual plants at the right size and shape: we trim ornamental shrubs like loropetalum, gardenia, azalea, and holly based on each species' growth pattern and bloom cycle. Trimming at the wrong time (say, cutting spring-blooming azaleas in late winter) removes flower buds and wastes a season of color, so we schedule around each plant's needs rather than a blanket pass. Bush shaping goes further, giving hedges defined geometric or natural forms (flat-topped privacy hedges, rounded boxwoods, tiered plantings) using gas-powered trimmers for bulk work and hand pruners for detail. Shaping visits typically run two to four times per year depending on species and desired density.

## Pruning and rejuvenation

Pruning is corrective work: removing dead, damaged, or crossing branches to improve health, airflow, and structure, which matters in Charleston's humid summers when poor airflow invites fungal disease. Rejuvenation pruning is a separate category: overgrown hedges neglected for years sometimes need hard cuts back to 6–12 inches, but not every species tolerates it. Ligustrum and loropetalum respond well; boxwood and some hollies do not. A general rule we follow: never remove more than one-third of a shrub's growth in a single session, which stresses the plant and can cause dieback.
