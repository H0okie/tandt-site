## Custom design and garden bed build-out

Good bed design accounts for more than looks — we evaluate sun and shade patterns, drainage, soil composition, and how the bed interacts with your home's architecture, then design plant selections, color sequencing by bloom season, and dimensions scaled to the space. Near the harbor and coast we factor in salt exposure; inland neighborhoods like West Ashley and Summerville allow a wider range. The physical build-out covers bed edging, turf and debris removal, grading, soil amendment, and planting, finished with mulch or pine straw to retain moisture and suppress weeds — for new construction we coordinate with grading and irrigation timelines, and for existing homes we work around mature trees and hardscape.

## Soil preparation

Charleston's native soil is predominantly sandy and drains quickly — an advantage against root rot, but nutrients wash out fast. Before any planting we amend the soil with organic matter and compost to improve water retention and nutrient availability, test pH, and adjust as needed. Many Lowcountry soils run slightly acidic, which suits azaleas and camellias but may require lime for other species. Proper bed prep is the single biggest factor in whether plants establish strong roots or struggle through their first summer.

## Annual and perennial plantings

Annuals deliver bold, immediate color — warm-season varieties like petunias, zinnias, lantana, and pentas plant from late March through May; cool-season pansies, snapdragons, and ornamental kale go in from October through early December. We offer one-time seasonal color installs and recurring annual rotation programs with priority scheduling. Perennial beds provide long-term structure and reduce replanting costs — we install proven Lowcountry perennials like knockout roses, daylilies, black-eyed Susans, muhly grass, and Confederate jasmine, layered by mature height and bloom timing for staggered interest from spring through fall.

## Frequently asked questions

### How do you prepare soil for flower beds in Charleston?

We test pH, amend with organic matter and compost to improve water retention, and adjust for slightly acidic Lowcountry soils — the foundation for strong root establishment in sandy native soil.

### What's the best time to plant annual flowers here?

Warm-season annuals (petunias, zinnias, lantana) plant from late March through May; cool-season annuals (pansies, snapdragons) go in from October through early December.

### Are perennials better than annuals for Charleston properties?

Perennial beds offer long-term structure and reduce yearly replanting costs. Lowcountry perennials like knockout roses, daylilies, and muhly grass tolerate the heat and humidity while providing staggered blooms.

### How does salt exposure affect design near the harbor?

Coastal properties need salt-tolerant selections, which we factor in during design; inland areas like West Ashley or Summerville allow wider plant variety.
