## Professional lawn fertilizer service

We apply professional-grade, slow-release fertilizers using a balanced N-P-K blend selected for your grass type. Slow-release granular formulas feed the turf over several weeks — which matters in Charleston's sandy soils, where water-soluble nutrients wash out fast after heavy rain. Our crews use calibrated spreaders for even distribution, avoiding the striping and burn spots we regularly see on lawns that came to us after DIY attempts or inconsistent service.

## Customized, soil-test-based programs

Every program starts with a property assessment — grass species, compaction, drainage — plus a recommended soil test through Clemson University's Agricultural Service Laboratory. That test tells us pH, organic matter, and existing nutrient levels, and drives every decision. A centipede lawn on James Island at pH 5.8 needs a very different blend than a Bermuda lawn in Mount Pleasant at 6.5. We build the plan around what the soil actually needs.

## Annual plan and coastal adjustments

Our annual plans run early spring through early fall, timed to soil temperature rather than the calendar:

- **Early spring (Mar–Apr):** first feeding as soil hits 65°F, with pre-emergent weed control.
- **Late spring (May–Jun):** higher-nitrogen application to support active growth.
- **Mid-summer (Jul–Aug):** lighter slow-release feeding to avoid heat stress.
- **Early fall (Sep):** final application to strengthen roots before dormancy.

For Mount Pleasant and other coastal properties, we raise potassium to improve salt tolerance and watch iron and manganese, which tend to run low in East Cooper soils.

## Matched to warm-season grasses

Charleston lawns are almost all warm-season: St. Augustine, centipede, Bermuda, and Zoysia. St. Augustine does well with 2–4 lbs of nitrogen per 1,000 sq ft annually; centipede needs far less (over-fertilizing it is one of the fastest ways to kill it); Bermuda benefits from higher nitrogen, which is why it's common on commercial and athletic turf. We match product, rate, and timing to the grass on your property.

## Frequently asked questions

### When is the best time to fertilize a lawn in Charleston?

Start warm-season grasses in early spring, once soil temperatures consistently reach 65°F. Most lawns benefit from 3–4 applications from March through September, with a final feeding in early fall before dormancy.

### What type of fertilizer works best here?

Slow-release granular fertilizers with a balanced N-P-K ratio. Sandy Lowcountry soils lose nutrients quickly, so slow-release formulas feed gradually and reduce runoff into local waterways.

### Do I need a soil test before fertilizing?

We strongly recommend one — it tells us pH, organic matter, and existing nutrient levels so the program is built on real data rather than guesswork.
