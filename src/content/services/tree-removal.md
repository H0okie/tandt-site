## Tree removal and cutting service

Our standard removal covers healthy trees that need to come down for construction, sight-line clearance, root damage to foundations, or redesign. We cut, section, and remove the tree, then grind the stump flush or below grade, with cleanup included — we leave the site clear of debris, sawdust, and chips unless you want them kept for mulch. Where full removal isn't practical in one pass, our tree cutting service takes down specific limbs, reduces canopy height, or sections a tree in stages — common for large live oaks overhanging roofs. On a James Island lot with a sprawling oak three feet from the house, we use controlled cutting and rigging to lower it piece by piece rather than dropping the whole tree.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a permit to remove a tree in Charleston?

It depends on zoning and tree size. Trees under 24 inches D.B.H. on single-family or duplex lots generally don't require a permit; commercially zoned lots and properties with three or more dwelling units must maintain 15 Protected Trees per acre. The City requires a tree inspection request before issuing a removal permit, and we can help you understand what applies.

### What's the cheapest time of year for tree removal?

Late fall and winter are typically least expensive — demand is lower and deciduous trees without foliage are faster to remove. That said, dead or hazardous trees should come out as soon as possible, especially before hurricane season in June.

### Can you remove a tree close to my house or power lines?

Yes — our crews have experience in tight spaces near homes, fences, and utility lines, using controlled cutting and proper rigging. For trees near power lines, coordination with the utility may be required, and we handle that communication.
