Charleston's warm, humid climate is great for growing grass — and for growing weeds, pests, and fungus right along with it. Timing your lawn care to the season is the difference between a yard that struggles and one that stays thick and green.

## Spring (March–May)

As the grass wakes up, this is the time for a pre-emergent weed treatment before summer weeds germinate, the first fertilization of the year, and a return to regular mowing. It's also the ideal window for aeration and overseeding if your lawn thinned out over winter.

## Summer (June–August)

Growth is at its peak, so weekly mowing keeps everything crisp. Mow a little higher in the heat to shade the soil and hold moisture, water deeply but less often, and keep an eye out for insect damage and brown patch.

## Fall (September–November)

Cooler weather is perfect for renovation work, new plantings, and a fall fertilization to strengthen roots before dormancy. Leaf and debris cleanup keeps the lawn breathing.

## Winter (December–February)

Growth slows, so service shifts to biweekly. It's the right time for pruning, hardscape projects, and planning next year's landscaping while the yard is dormant.
